string (n.) — a thread that ties two things together.

Most dating apps profit from keeping you single.

Subscription apps earn more the longer you swipe. String earns when venues pay for placement — not a subscription from you, and not a percentage of what you spend. Nothing about our business gets better the longer you're single.

The problem

The incentive is the product.

When an app makes money from monthly subscriptions, your endless search is the business model. Matches that go nowhere aren't a bug — they're retention. We built String around a different incentive: venues pay us, not you — and they only keep paying if real dates keep happening.

The old model

A subscription app earns while you swipe, message, and renew. A date that actually happens is, to them, a churned customer. The math rewards keeping you in the app.

The String model

Venues pay us a placement fee to be featured in recommendations — not a cut of your date, and not a fee that scales with what you spend. But venues only keep paying if String is actually sending them dates. Stop delivering, and they stop renewing. So the business still depends on dates happening — just not one transaction at a time.

The difference

What that alignment changes

Scarcity over volume

Ten active matches, not an infinite deck. The cap makes every connection worth real attention.

Momentum over limbo

A fourteen-day expiry keeps things moving. Matches become dates — or make room for ones that will.

Places over profiles

Every date lands at a vetted Chicago venue, chosen for first-date energy — not left to "idk, wherever."

Minutes over hours

We measure success in dates that happen, not time on screen. String is built to be closed.
